Bike Share Insights: Seven Tableau Views for an Operations Team

Course
OPAN 6600 Visualization & Storytelling with Data, Georgetown MSBA (Fall 2026)
Type
Individual final assignment
Tools
Tableau (calculated fields, parameters, FIXED LOD expressions, maps, treemaps)
Data
78,704 trips across 63 stations in the San Francisco Bay Area bike share system

Project folder on GitHub

The headline

This bike share runs on commuters riding the last mile to and from the train. Transit hubs are 11 of 63 stations but start 24% of trips, the busiest single dock beats four whole cities combined, and casual riders go over the time limit 20 times more often than subscribers. Each view below ends with a specific action for the operator.

The seven views

1. When do people ride? Weekday commute peaks

Two dark bands, 8 to 10am and 5 to 7pm on weekdays. Tuesday at 8am is the hottest cell, with 1,672 trips. Weekends barely register.

A data fix first: the start times were stored in UTC, which put the morning rush at 1pm. I built a Local Start Time calculated field that shifts each timestamp to local clock time, daylight saving included, and checked it against the date column on all 78,704 rows. Action: do rebalancing and repairs midday or on weekends, and have docks full before 8am.

2. Transit hubs drive demand

A Station Type calculated field tags each start station as Caltrain, BART, Ferry Building or other. Only 11 of 63 stations are transit hubs, but they start 18,990 trips (24%). The biggest bubble, San Francisco Caltrain 2 (330 Townsend), has 7,904 trips: more than San Jose, Palo Alto, Redwood City and Mountain View combined (2,535). Action: put the next station next to a train platform.

3. Rain hurts ridership, temperature doesn't

Each dot is one weekday (weekends removed with a Day Type calc). Across 41°F to 77°F the correlation between temperature and trips is 0.02. Rainy weekdays average about 119 trips against 131 on clear days, roughly 9% fewer. The outliers are holidays and the December 11, 2014 storm (3.12 inches of rain), which cut ridership to 18.

4. Tourists stick to the waterfront

A % Customer Trips calc (one-time pass riders over all trips) on a San Francisco map. Systemwide, casual customers are 3.0% of rides. At Embarcadero at Sansome it's 18.9%, more than six times as much, and the top four stations on this metric all line the Embarcadero. The five busiest SoMa stations sit at 3.3% or below. Action: day-pass marketing belongs on the waterfront, not in SoMa.

5. Reverse commuters: San Francisco to Mountain View

Every trip is tagged with the 94107 zip code (around the SF Caltrain station), yet 1,797 trips happen in Mountain View, 35 miles south, and 99% fall on weekdays. A Caltrain Direction calc shows that between 7 and 10am, 99% of rides start at a Caltrain dock, and between 4 and 7pm, 97% end at one. People take the train down, ride the last mile to the office, and reverse it at night.

6. Casual riders blow past the 30-minute limit

An Overtime Limit parameter (default 30 minutes) drives a % Over Limit calc. 18.3% of casual rides go over, against 0.9% of subscriber rides, about 20 times more often. At a 60-minute limit the gap gets wider (10.7% vs. 0.4%). Subscribers' median ride is 7 minutes; casual riders' is 15. Action: spell out overtime fees on the pricing screen. Many casual riders probably don't know about them.

7. A few routes carry the system

A Route calc joins start and end stations, and a Min Trips per Route parameter drives a filter built on a FIXED LOD expression. Of 1,287 routes, just 33 carry almost 30% of all trips. 20 of those 33 touch Caltrain. Return trips show an imbalance: riders heading to the train pick the 4th Street Caltrain dock far more often, and it takes in about 3,000 more bikes than it sends out. Action: start the rebalancing trucks' day at 4th Street.

Techniques used

  • Calculated fields: Local Start Time (UTC conversion), Station Type, Day Type, Weather Type, % Customer Trips, Caltrain Direction, % Over Limit, Route
  • Parameters: Overtime Limit, Min Trips per Route
  • Level of detail: a FIXED LOD filter for route volume
  • Chart types: heat map, packed bubbles, scatter, symbol map, stacked bars, bar comparison, treemap
