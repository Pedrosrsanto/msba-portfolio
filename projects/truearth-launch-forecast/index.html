Should TruEarth Launch a Fresh Pizza? A Demand Forecast

The decision

TruEarth's whole-grain fresh pasta kit, Cucina Fresca, was a hit: $35M in retail sales in 2007, close to its forecast's "excellent" scenario. Now the company is weighing a whole-grain refrigerated pizza. It needs $12M in wholesale sales to justify the launch, and a competitor, Rigazzi, is already testing one.

Recommendation: launch, but not as a copy of the pasta rollout

At the 11% penetration the research found, pizza clears the $12M hurdle in all three product-quality scenarios ($12.2M, $15.0M and $17.0M). The only way it misses is low penetration and weak repeat purchasing at the same time.

Why pizza is harder than pasta

On paper, pizza looks better: a bigger category ($5.8B vs. $4.4B), packaging equipment already paid for, and a lower volume requirement ($12M vs. $14M). The concept test tells a different story:

  • Top-two-box purchase intent fell from 76% to 60%, below even the 62% norm for regular fresh pasta. "Definitely would buy" dropped from 27% to 18%
  • The average hides two groups. Existing TruEarth customers react almost like the pasta sample (79% top-two, 4.2 likeability). Non-customers are lukewarm (54%, 3.5), and they're 89% of target households but only 75% of the survey sample
  • Price is the top complaint. 27% found pizza too expensive, against 8% for pasta. After the home trial, even favorable testers would pay $10.75 on average against an expected $12.38, which is more than takeout ($11.72)

The forecast

Because customers and non-customers are too far apart to average, I ran the forecast separately for each segment instead of using one blended trial rate.

Non-customers TruEarth customers
Implied trial rate 23.7% 36.7%
Awareness 12% 50%

Applied to 58.8MM target households at 11% penetration and 40% ACV distribution, that gives 1.07MM trial households, 44% of them from the customer segment, who are only 11% of the market. With 49% of triers buying twice more:

  • 2.12MM total purchases
  • $26.2M at retail, $17.0M at wholesale: 42% above the $12M requirement

Sensitivity

I tested penetration from 5% to 15% against three repeat-purchase scenarios. Six of nine cases clear $12M. Break-even penetration is 2.3% for an excellent product, 5.2% for an average one and 10.5% for a mediocre one.

How I'd launch it

  1. Start in the priority cities, where penetration is closer to 13%, and aim early marketing at Cucina Fresca buyers, the most aware and most willing segment
  2. Fix price before launch. Even at a $10.75 average spend, base-case wholesale sales would still be about $14.8M, but margin per kit needs a second look
  3. Fix the crust. 20% of unfavorable triers asked for a less chewy one, and the forecast depends on repeat purchases
  4. Set a stop rule: if repeat purchases in the first markets come in under 20%, hold off on wider distribution
  5. Don't give up the head start. Being first helped pasta, and Rigazzi is already testing
